For the three-dimensional incompressible Navier–Stokes equations, the vorticity stretching and transport terms can be compressed into the curl of the solenoidal Lamb vector \(L=\mathbb P(u\times\omega)\). This paper studies a signed Littlewood–Paley shell injection built from that representation. The associated vertical heat extension has an unconditional bulk estimate: after normalization by enstrophy, its full heat-height integral belongs to \(L^1\) in physical time for every periodic Leray–Hopf solution on a finite interval. The estimate persists under bounded-overlap spatial localization and yields a conditional local continuation criterion.

The bulk estimate does not recover the bottom trace. A fixed smooth radial tight frame and an explicit global smooth 2D3C Navier–Stokes family along dyadic frequencies \(K=2^j\) give an exact bottom-to-bulk factor \(2K^2\). The same family shows that matched partitions do not hide the loss, while a covariant interior-cylinder construction rules out every geometry-only factor \(o(r^{-2})\). A second construction uses the finite-dimensional implicit-function theorem for the exact Navier–Stokes flow to prescribe a positive-time full-shell zero with nonzero nonlinear forcing. After an amplitude-frequency scaling, its scale-invariant entry atom is of order \(\lambda^{-4}\), whereas the bare normalized Leray–Lamb time budget is of order \(\lambda^{-6}\). Thus no uniform bare payment of such internal entries is possible. An outgoing coarea identity gives an exact scale-matched representation of every finite-order entry, but its zero-level occupation density is not controlled here. None of the results proves global regularity or constructs a singularity. Computer-assisted finite Fourier, spectral-reconstruction, and Galerkin calculations are used as audits and corroboration; no analytic theorem is inferred from floating-point output.
