Heat evolution of a high-degree Chebyshev polynomial near an endpoint has two logarithmic scales. For \(Q_{m,A}(s,c)=(e^{sA^{-2}D^2}T_m)(1+c/A)\) and \(\mu=(m^2/A)^{1/3}\), the common amplitude has scale \(\mu^2\), while a bounded endpoint displacement has scale \(\mu\). I give uniform, nonoptimal error bounds on compact positive time intervals throughout \(\mu\to\infty\), \(\mu/A\to0\). The relative-displacement estimate follows from a direct Gaussian tilt, so its error is not obtained by subtracting two coarse amplitude estimates. An edge-profile proposition applies also to the endpoint-normalized Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind.

An explicit sequence of consecutive-integer-frequency shears transfers the first-kind profile over the full time interval, using one initial datum for each member of the sequence. Its signed collar-flux quotient retains an \(\exp(\Theta(\mu))\) edge factor with algebraic factors stated separately. The denominator is the cubic mass of the nonconstant component on the whole spatial plateau. For the stated amplitude normalization, inclusion of the constant drift in the denominator instead makes this quotient decay; amplitude rescaling and changes of observation frame are treated explicitly. A supporting time-weighted inequality is a corollary of Erdélyi’s results, and an elementary \(N\)-term construction shows its \(N^{4/3}\) order cannot be reduced in the abstract temporal class. The spatial extrapolation scale and polynomial heat operation are established inputs. The shear application concerns smooth exact solutions and gives no general Navier–Stokes regularity conclusion.
