  Regularity for axisymmetric Navier-Stokes with an Euler length

                                  Peter Constantin, Mihaela Ignatova, and Vlad Vicol


           Abstract.We prove local regularity for axisymmetric suitable weak solutions of the 3D Navier-
           Stokes equations, which are smooth before the terminal timet= 0, and satisfy Type II pointwise
           boundsatavanishinglengthscaleℓ(t). Wesayℓ(t)isanEuler lengthifitisnon-increasing,satisfies
           a doubling condition, and ifℓ(t)→0 and (−t)/ℓ(t)                                   2 →0 ast→0          −.  This includes power laws
           ℓ(t) = (−t)           γ with 0< γ <1/2, and logarithmic enlargements of the parabolic length.  Our main
           result shows that local bounds of the type|u(·,t)|≤Cℓ(t)/(−t) and|∇                                              2u(·,t)|≤C/((−t)ℓ(t)) for
           allt∈(−1,0) imply regularity. The proof adapts the circulation and potential-vorticity argument
           of our earlier paper [4] to ancient limits obtained from the rescaled vorticity system by zooming in.
           We show that the second derivative a priori assumption may be replaced by a H¨older bound on the
           azimuthal vorticity and a corresponding bound on its potential vorticity.

    1.2. Prior results.In the absence of swirl, global regularity for the axisymmetric 3D Navier-
Stokes equations was established by Ladyzhenskaya [10] and by Ukhovskii and Yudovich [26].
    In the presence of swirl, the regularity of axisymmetric solutions to 3D Navier-Stokes has been
established under the additional hypothesis of a Type I bound, an assumption which is invariant
under parabolic scaling.   Chen, Strain, Yau, and Tsai [2] proved regularity of the solution at
t= 0 assuming the pointwise bound|u| ≤C(r                        2−t)    −1/2     .  Chen, Strain, Tsai, and Yau [3] and
Koch, Nadirashvili, Seregin, and ˇSver´ak [9] proved regularity assuming thatr|u|≤C. Seregin and
ˇSver´ak [21] imposed a temporal Type I bound on the meridional velocity alone:|b|≤C(−t)                           −1/2     .
All of these proofs rely on the structure of the equations satisfied by the circulation and by the
potential vorticity Ω =ω   θ/r. We refer to the book of Tsai [25, Chapter 10] for a detailed account
of the many conditional regularity results for solutions of 3D axisymmetric Navier-Stokes.
    Escauriaza,Seregin,andˇSver´ak[6,Theorem1.4]provedtheregularityofsuitableweaksolutions
which are bounded in the scale-invariant normL        ∞t L3x; their argument is by contradiction and gives
no quantitative bound.  Tao [24] made this result quantitative for classical solutions onR           3 and
obtained a quantitative lower bound, of triple logarithmic type, for the blowup rate of theL      3 norm
at a finite time singularity, along a sequence of times approaching that singularity. Subsequently,
Palasek [14] replaced the triple logarithm by a double logarithm, for the weighted critical norms
∥r  1−3/q    u∥  Lq,bothforaxisymmetricsolutionswithq∈(2,3]andforsolutionswithoutanysymmetry
withq∈(3,∞).  O˙za´nski and Palasek [13] then bounded every derivative of an axisymmetric
solution by a double exponential of its weakL      3 norm. Seregin [15] proved that at a singular axis
point the three scale-invariant local energy quantities arising in the theory of partial regularity
all have an infinite upper limit, so that an axisymmetric singularity must be Type II also in the
local-energy sense. Seregin also formulated Type II blowup scenarios through such weighted scale-
invariant quantities and studied them in [16,17,18,19].
    The argument in this paper is inspired by the axisymmetric Euler profile theorem that we
have established in our earlier work [4, Theorem 4.5]. We adapt the potential-vorticity transport
along backward meridional trajectories, from the proof of [4, Theorem 4.5], to ancient limits of the
rescaled vorticity system; here the circulation is eliminated by the rescaling instead.  The type of
argument employed here, in which we zoom in at a sequence of points and times where a desired
bound fails, and apply a rigidity statement to the resulting ancient limit, is the one through which
Type I singularities were excluded previously [9,21]. We emphasize that in this paper we do not
assume a self-similar ansatz for the solution.
